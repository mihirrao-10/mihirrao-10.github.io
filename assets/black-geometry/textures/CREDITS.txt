Surface maps converted to grayscale from NASA imagery.
Only the Moon and Sun fields are active on the current homepage. All artwork
is grayscale, without visible wireframes or tessellated surface treatments.
The fictional planet uses an authored terrain/cloud field, not an Earth map.

Archived Earth field (not loaded by the current homepage): NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio.
Blue Marble data courtesy of Reto Stockli (NASA/GSFC) and NASA Earth Observatory.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/2915/
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a002900/a002915/bluemarble-2048.png

Moon: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit.
Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera / Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004700/a004720/lroc_color_2k.jpg

Files: 4-byte little-endian uint16 width and height, followed by 8-bit grayscale pixels.
Gzip files preserve exactly the same payload.

Galaxy fields: grayscale luminance conversion, background removal, and soft edge
feathering. The current Milky Way is authored procedural geometry rather than an
image-derived field. Its broad bar/arm structure references NASA's illustration:
https://science.nasa.gov/resource/the-milky-way-galaxy/
Luminous features in the Abell 1689 field supplied the earlier population of
112 galaxies, retained outside the active build. Current galaxy scenes use
authored three-dimensional star populations, not photographic display planes.
The final scene is now a globular star cluster. Its radial profile, stellar
positions, and motion are authored. Dark ink represents luminous density.
Source frames are not time series.

Reproduction of Sun, archived Saturn/M51, and cluster fields: save the four inputs
as sun.jpg, saturn.jpg, galaxy.jpg, cluster.jpg; verify the hashes above, then run
tools/black-geometry/prepare-astronomy.py with the input directory. The SDO latest
URL is mutable: use the recorded frame. Requires Pillow and NumPy; the website
build does not. The active map manifest excludes archived fields.

The archived neutron star used authored dipole geometry and polar beams:
https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/neutron_stars1.html
The current black hole traces deflected rays through an authored turbulent
accretion disk around an absorbing center. It is an approximate illustration,
not a relativistic geodesic solver. The accretion silhouette references
Gargantua from Interstellar and NASA's warped-world visualization:
https://www.nasa.gov/universe/nasa-visualization-shows-a-black-holes-warped-world/

The two user-supplied images in Documents guide the black-hole and globular-
cluster compositions only. Neither reference image nor any film still is
copied into the website or redistributed. The radial stellar cloud, Milky Way,
colliding galaxy pair, and globular cluster are authored particle populations;
their depth, orbital rates, and interaction are not measured or simulated gravity.
